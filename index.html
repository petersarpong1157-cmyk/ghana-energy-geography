Renewable Energy Opportunity and the Geography of Infrastructure Disadvantage

Settlement-level evidence from Ghana on modeled relative wealth, transmission and road proximity, and long-term solar photovoltaic potential.

Research question

Does the geography of solar opportunity align with socioeconomic and infrastructure disadvantage?

Using the World Bank Distributed Renewable Energy (DRE) Atlas settlement data, the study documents how modeled relative wealth co-varies with distance to transmission lines and roads, settlement size, and long-run solar PV potential. The analysis compares region and district fixed effects and evaluates geographic dependence in model residuals.

Important distinction: Distance to transmission lines does not establish electricity connection or service quality. Solar potential does not measure installed capacity or actual generation. All relationships here are observational, not causal.

Key findings

Infrastructure proximity

The lowest observed relative-wealth quintile has a median distance of 13.1 km to transmission lines, compared with 5.4 km for the highest quintile.

Stable conditional associations

Transmission-line and road-distance coefficients remain negative across the tested fixed-effects and geographic-inference specifications.

Solar uncertainty

Solar–wealth coefficients are sensitive to spatial controls and inference bandwidths. The findings do not support a universally robust solar–wealth conclusion.

Methods and robustness

Spatial model comparison

Region and district fixed effects, additional latitude/longitude trends, and geographically clustered uncertainty.

Spatial dependence

Residual Moran's I was 0.352 with region controls and 0.285 with district controls (999-permutation tests, p = 0.001).

Geographic harmonization

Alternative 261- and 272-polygon district assignments were compared against source district classifications on a shared sample of 21,576 settlements.

Research integrity and reproducibility

The principal region-fixed-effects regression was independently reproduced in Python from the settlement CSV. Phase 6–9 numerical outputs were reconciled. The complete Conley and polygon pipeline has not been independently reproduced end-to-end.

  • Modeled Relative Wealth Index may share underlying geospatial/connectivity predictors with infrastructure measures; exact overlap remains unverified.
  • Official validity dates of the supplied district boundary polygons remain unconfirmed.
  • 3,735 source settlements lack a wealth estimate; selection bias remains possible.
  • Significant residual spatial dependence persists after geographic fixed effects.
  • Solar PV potential is a long-term modeled resource estimate, not an investment or electricity-access measure.
